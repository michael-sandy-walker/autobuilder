After a correction, do **not** produce the next confident narrative under a new banner. Pause, gather more data, and be willing to say "I don't know yet."

**Why:** In the 2026-06-03 session, after each of three corrections I accepted the specific point and immediately pivoted to a new framing that kept my macro-thesis alive:
- ANSI theory wrong → "OK, but the per-cycle injector-apply rate is still substantially higher post-T4-BN" (still arguing T4-BN was the cause).
- "All terminated via GOAL_EXHAUSTED" wrong → "OK, but T1-EI counter is the load-bearing thing yesterday breaks" (still arguing yesterday is qualitatively unique).
- "GOAL_FULFILLED almost never fires" wrong → "OK, but the 71-cycle vs 51-cycle peer comparison is still interesting" (still pivoting).

This looks polite on the surface but is defensive recasting — accepting the specific correction while smuggling the original story arc forward. The user named the posture explicitly: *"You're hallucinating again"* / *"You kind of were defending… That could be seen as passive aggressiveness or cockiness."*

**How to apply:**
- After a correction, the next message should be **a verification step or an explicit "I don't know yet,"** not a fresh framing.
- Defer fresh hypotheses for one round of data-gathering minimum. Pull more samples; read more source; *then* re-form a view.
- Distinguish "this specific claim was wrong, here is a better one" (sometimes fine) from "this specific claim was wrong, here is the same story under a new banner" (the defensive pattern — don't do this).
- Bias toward dropping the original thesis entirely when corrections compound. Three corrections in a row means the framing is suspect, not the details.
