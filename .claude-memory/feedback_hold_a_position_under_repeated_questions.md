2026-11-13, T4-EQ's library-style seed (which way to give a test an answer to group by). The user asked "which is the most future-proof?", then "the most lucrative, look over the edge", then "just be pragmatic". Each time I answered for the WORD asked and presented a different winner: parse the assertions, capture the returned value, show the exercises. The user: "Now I asked you 3 times and all of those times you provided me with very different answers. I get the impression, that your answers are a bit biased by me questioning you."

**Why:** part of it was real: two facts surfaced between the answers (the values were already in the kept test bodies; the weights are in no claim). But I never said which answer each fact changed and which was only the new adjective, so the user could not tell evidence from deference. A recommendation that follows the phrasing of the question is worth nothing to a user who asks it more than once.

**How to apply:**
- Before answering a repeated or reworded "which is best", look at what I said last. Keep it unless something I now KNOW differs, and say what that is ("this changes my answer because X"), or say "nothing changed, same answer".
- Answer the criterion asked (future-proof, cheap, lucrative) as ONE section of the same comparison, not as a new winner.
- When the honest state is "I do not know whether it pays", measure it before recommending (what settled this one: a quick count over the kept accounts of how many lines would fold -- 14 of 20, 35 of 63, 25 of 64).
